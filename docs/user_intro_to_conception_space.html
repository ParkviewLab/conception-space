conception-space

A place to think with the notes you already have

You don't need another notes app. You need somewhere to think with the notes you've got.

If you use Obsidian, Joplin, Notion, or just a folder of Markdown, you already have the hard part: a corpus of your own ideas. What you don't have is a way to see its shape. Folders flatten it. Search finds a note but not its neighbourhood. The graph view is a pretty hairball you glance at and close. The relationships between your ideas live only in your head — and heads forget.

conception-space is a desktop app that turns your knowledge into a place: a 3D space you build by hand, where a point can be one of your real notes, and where you put it is what it means.

Try it
01

Made of real notes, not a new format.

A node in the space can be a handle onto a real file: a Markdown note or a PDF. Open a node and you are reading and editing the actual note, in the plain, open formats you already write in. The space isn't a diagram about your notes; the points that hold files are your notes, arranged so you can fly through them and think.

02

You place it by hand — and the placing is the thinking.

This is the opposite of an auto-generated graph. No layout algorithm decides where things go; you do. Putting this note near that one and far from those is a claim: these belong together; that one doesn't. By the time the space looks right, you have thought the relationships through. The arrangement isn't a picture of your understanding — it is your understanding, made of where things sit.

03

See the structure. Don't reconstruct it.

A list makes you read relationships one at a time and rebuild the whole shape in your head. A space hands the whole shape to your eyes at once: how dense a region is, which connections are heavy, what clusters and what stands alone. Your visual system was built for exactly this. It is a far higher-bandwidth way to grasp how a hundred ideas relate than any list can ever be.

04

A place you can come back to.

Because you placed everything, it is spatially memorable — "the dense cluster, upper left, is my thesis material." And it lasts across sessions: leave it, return months later, and the placement itself reminds you what you were thinking when you put that there. It is a workshop you keep, not a chart you screenshot.

05

Discover what you didn't know was there.

Arrange enough, and structure you never typed begins to show: clusters you didn't plan, gaps where a connection is missing, bridges between fields you thought were separate. The eye catches the pattern before you can put it into words — and that noticing is often the insight you were missing.

The honest specifics

  • Free and open source (AGPL-3.0-or-later). Your knowledge, your tool.
  • macOS, Windows, and Linux. The macOS build (Apple Silicon) is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized, so it opens without a warning.
  • Plain, open formats — your notes are Markdown and PDF, the same kinds you already write.
  • A real-time 3D space (built on Three.js), not a static diagram: fly through it, save viewpoints, and return to them.
  • Early. The app draws a space you author by hand and lets you fly through it. Point-and-place authoring, a room-scale version for Apple Vision Pro, and how the app meets the notes you already keep are ideas under consideration, listed in the in-flight ideas.
